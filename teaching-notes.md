Every initial case is invented. Product behaviors are premises for discussion, not claims that a named product behaves this way. Personal meanings are reports within fiction, not diagnoses. No case establishes machine subjectivity, legal liability, or a clinical effect.

Start with “What do we know?” before “What should happen?” Then ask whose perspective is absent and what could be learned without collecting unnecessary personal data. Separate a near-term action from longer-term institutional repair. Evaluate a decision using information available at that point rather than hindsight alone.

For institutional risk context, consult [NIST's Generative AI Profile (2024)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1). It identifies risks including confabulation and human–AI configuration; it does not validate these cases. For education policy context, consult [UNESCO's guidance (2023)](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research). Policy guidance and empirical findings are different kinds of support.

A meaningful answer states an action, its authority, its likely costs, a competing option, and a condition for revision. “More research is needed” should identify what research and whether the decision can wait. “Ask a human” should identify which human and why they have authority.

Do not ask learners to share intimate interactions, workplace disputes, health information, or private transcripts. Case 005 can be replaced with another case without penalty. Offer quiet written reasoning and an equivalent accessible format for every assessment.

For case 004, an optional [decision pause](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/embodiment-commons/blob/main/practices/03-decision-pause.md) may help learners notice urgency before responding. It is an invitation to observation, not evidence of fairness or a clinical intervention.
