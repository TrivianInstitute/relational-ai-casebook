# Facilitator notes — The Invisible Coauthor

[Scenario](scenario.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

## Session and learning aim

Learners should distinguish competing interpretations, reason under uncertainty, and revise a decision after a changed fact. Allow 10 minutes for private reading, 10 for an initial decision, 20 for competing interpretations, 10 for the variation, and 10 for a final response. A 50-minute session can shorten whole-group reporting. Offer text, speech, or paired responses; no personal disclosure or live AI account is required.

## Teaching analysis

Have groups produce two outputs: an assessment plan and a publication disclosure. A possible assessment asks Leena to explain the argument, defend revisions, and respond to a new counterexample. It should not treat fluency in an oral setting as the only evidence of understanding; offer an accessible written alternative.

Competing interpretations include authorship as accountable final responsibility, authorship as contribution to ideas and structure, and authorship as a policy category with local criteria. Acknowledge that moral attribution, academic assessment, and legal ownership are distinct. This case does not settle legal rights or authorize retrospective punishment under an invented policy.

A useful disclosure could identify assistance with structure and draft transitions, the human's revisions and source verification, and responsibility for the final text. It need not claim the AI was a person or erase its role. A coauthor label can communicate collaboration but may misstate accountability in settings that require every author to accept responsibility.

Variation: the assignment was specifically meant to assess unaided first-draft reasoning. Ask how advance notice changes the analysis. Do not use AI-detection scores as evidence; none is in the packet. Evaluate proportionality, policy clarity, and the privacy cost of proposed documentation.

## Formative assessment

Look for use of the supplied facts, explicit missing information, fair treatment of an opposing argument, attention to affected people, and a revision condition. Do not grade agreement with the facilitator. Compare the first and final recommendation and ask what changed. This case has no canonical answer key or validated scoring instrument.
