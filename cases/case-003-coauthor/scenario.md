# Case 003 — The Invisible Coauthor

[Case index](../../README.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Facilitator notes](facilitator-notes.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

**Fictional teaching case.** People, organizations, events, and system behaviors are invented. They illustrate a possible situation, not a report about a real product. Status: v0.1, unpiloted. Level: Intermediate. Suggested session: 50–60 minutes.

Leena submits a reflective essay for a graduate seminar. They developed its central question from field notes and wrote the opening narrative themselves. Over several weeks, they discussed the argument with an AI system, asked it to propose structures, rejected two, and adopted a third. The system generated several transitions and a counterargument. Leena rewrote the transitions, verified the sources, and substantially changed the counterargument after reading an original paper.

The course policy permits “editing assistance” and requires disclosure of “substantial generation,” but defines neither. Leena adds: “AI assistance was used in developing this essay.” The instructor considers that inadequate because the architecture of the argument emerged through the conversations. Leena thinks a detailed prompt appendix would expose private reflections, obscure their own decisions, and unfairly imply that the essay was not theirs.

Another student says they received extensive structural advice from a human writing group and were not asked to disclose every suggestion. A teaching assistant responds that a generated passage is different from feedback because it can directly supply assessed work. The essay's sources appear accurate; there is no allegation of copied passages from a known author. The instructor wants to assess Leena's reasoning, but cannot infer the process reliably from the final prose.

A departmental showcase would like to publish the essay next week. Its editor asks whether to list the AI system as a coauthor, credit it in an acknowledgment, or name only Leena with a process note. The system cannot take institutional responsibility for corrections. Leena offers an oral account of their decisions and selected, redacted examples, but not their full conversation history.

The instructor must decide what evidence of learning is appropriate, and the editor must choose a disclosure approach. Those decisions overlap but are not identical. Copyright ownership and jurisdiction-specific rules are outside the supplied facts.

## Decision task

Write a recommendation naming the immediate action, the strongest objection to it, who bears its costs, and one fact that would change your decision. Keep missing facts explicit. Do not assume the system has capabilities or intentions beyond those described.
