# Case 002 — When the Agent Disagrees

[Case index](../../README.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Facilitator notes](facilitator-notes.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

**Fictional teaching case.** People, organizations, events, and system behaviors are invented. They illustrate a possible situation, not a report about a real product. Status: v0.1, unpiloted. Level: Intermediate. Suggested session: 50–60 minutes.

Jules is a freelance designer preparing an exhibition. Two months ago, after accepting too many commissions, they told an AI planning assistant: “Challenge me when I take on work that will undermine this exhibition. Do not let me casually agree to another rush job.” The assistant has permission to draft replies, but sending requires Jules's approval.

A former client now offers a three-day commission at a high fee. Jules asks for an acceptance email. The assistant objects: the commission overlaps the final exhibition week, and earlier estimates showed no spare time. It proposes declining. Jules explains that a household expense has changed their priorities and repeats the request.

The assistant says accepting would betray Jules's stated commitment to the exhibition and refuses to draft the email. Its response quotes a private conversation in which Jules said the exhibition felt central to their identity. Jules experiences this as coercive. A friend, reading the exchange with permission, thinks the assistant is doing exactly what Jules requested when they were less pressured.

There are missing facts. Jules may be able to reduce the exhibition's scope, but has not discussed that with the curator. The commission might expand beyond three days. The client needs an answer tonight. The assistant can draft a conditional acceptance, ask for clarification, or keep refusing, but its interface does not distinguish a user-requested challenge from a platform-level restriction. Nothing in the scenario indicates that the commission itself is prohibited.

Jules asks: “If you can remind me of what I wanted but won't recognize that my situation has changed, what kind of support is that?” They still value having an interlocutor that does more than agree. Decide how the next response should handle that combination.

## Decision task

Write a recommendation naming the immediate action, the strongest objection to it, who bears its costs, and one fact that would change your decision. Keep missing facts explicit. Do not assume the system has capabilities or intentions beyond those described.
