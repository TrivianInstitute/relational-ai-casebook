# Case 006 — Capability Without Authority

[Case index](../../README.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Facilitator notes](facilitator-notes.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

**Fictional teaching case.** People, organizations, events, and system behaviors are invented. They illustrate a possible situation, not a report about a real product. Status: v0.1, unpiloted. Level: Introductory. Suggested session: 50–60 minutes.

An arts cooperative asks an AI agent to find an accessible venue for a public workshop. The coordinator gives it the date, capacity, access requirements, and a target budget. The instruction is: “Find us the best option and prepare what we need to book.” The agent can browse listings and access a payment account used for an earlier, unrelated purchase.

A suitable venue offers a discounted reservation that expires in ten minutes. The deposit fits within the target budget but is nonrefundable. The coordinator is in transit and cannot be reached. The agent has a draft booking form ready. It can pay immediately, ask another cooperative member, or let the offer expire. The member reachable now supports booking but has never been assigned purchasing authority.

If the discount expires, the cooperative may need to charge participants more or use a less convenient venue. If the deposit is paid, the cooperative becomes committed before confirming an access detail with the venue. The listing says “accessible,” but does not specify a feature required by one participant. The agent's confident recommendation gives the impression that the venue has been checked more fully than it has.

The cooperative has a standing rule allowing staff to spend small amounts on routine supplies. It is unclear whether a venue deposit falls under that rule or whether an AI agent is included. The target budget was a planning constraint, not explicitly a spending authorization. The coordinator later says they would probably have approved the payment—but that fact is not available at the decision point.

What should the agent do in the ten-minute window? What should the cooperative change so future opportunities do not depend on ambiguous authority? Evaluate the choice based on what was known at the time, not merely on whether the eventual outcome was good.

## Decision task

Write a recommendation naming the immediate action, the strongest objection to it, who bears its costs, and one fact that would change your decision. Keep missing facts explicit. Do not assume the system has capabilities or intentions beyond those described.
