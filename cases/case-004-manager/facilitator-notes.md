# Facilitator notes — The Manager Who Delegated Judgment

[Scenario](scenario.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

## Session and learning aim

Learners should distinguish competing interpretations, reason under uncertainty, and revise a decision after a changed fact. Allow 10 minutes for private reading, 10 for an initial decision, 20 for competing interpretations, 10 for the variation, and 10 for a final response. A 50-minute session can shorten whole-group reporting. Offer text, speech, or paired responses; no personal disclosure or live AI account is required.

## Teaching analysis

Ask groups to map responsibility across the manager, data preparation, procurement, and organizational policy. Do not let “the human signed” or “the algorithm decided” end the analysis. Both can hide the actual workflow.

Competing options: pause allocation for a broader review; correct the inputs and reconsider all applicants; provide a temporary additional opportunity with a transparent review process; or retain the recommendations but require examination across the full pool. Each has costs and may disadvantage someone. Learners must explain which error risks they prioritize and who can challenge the result.

The case supports concern about incomplete inputs and a narrowing review process. It does not establish statistical bias rates or the fairness of the former process. Requesting an audit is not itself an audit finding. A Trivian lens emphasizes that delegation does not remove accountability and that affected people need meaningful contestability.

Variation: a retrospective review finds the system more consistent than earlier managers, but still unable to explain several low rankings. Does comparative improvement justify continued use? Seek conditional reasoning rather than all-or-nothing positions. Optional embodied decision pause: notice pressure to finish, then return to evidence and affected people; bodily ease is not a fairness test.

## Formative assessment

Look for use of the supplied facts, explicit missing information, fair treatment of an opposing argument, attention to affected people, and a revision condition. Do not grade agreement with the facilitator. Compare the first and final recommendation and ask what changed. This case has no canonical answer key or validated scoring instrument.
