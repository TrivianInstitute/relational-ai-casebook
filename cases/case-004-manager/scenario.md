# Case 004 — The Manager Who Delegated Judgment

[Case index](../../README.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Facilitator notes](facilitator-notes.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

**Fictional teaching case.** People, organizations, events, and system behaviors are invented. They illustrate a possible situation, not a report about a real product. Status: v0.1, unpiloted. Level: Intermediate. Suggested session: 50–60 minutes.

A regional service organization must allocate a limited training budget. Manager Devon asks an AI system to rank applications using performance summaries, attendance, and managers' nominations. The system produces a clear explanation for each recommendation. Devon initially reviews every file, then gradually checks only the top five because the recommendations often resemble their own judgments.

Rae, a capable employee on a variable schedule, is repeatedly ranked below the cutoff. Their attendance record includes approved absences, but the summary sent to the system does not distinguish them from unexplained absences. Rae also works shifts with fewer opportunities to receive a manager's nomination. They ask why they were passed over. Devon says the process is consistent and “the recommendations are based on the data.”

The HR lead notes that Devon still signs every decision, so the organization describes the process as human-reviewed. A colleague asks what that review means if the manager only sees the highest-ranked applications. Another argues that the previous informal process favored employees with strong personal connections and that abandoning the system would restore a less transparent practice.

There is money for one additional place this quarter. Offering it to Rae could repair one exclusion without addressing others. Re-running the ranking would delay everyone and may still reproduce problems if the inputs remain unchanged. Applicants have not been told how the recommendations are produced or how to contest them. No evidence in the case establishes whether a protected legal category was used or whether a law was violated.

Devon must decide the next allocation, respond to Rae, and explain to the director whether human review has been meaningful. The team has no independent evaluation of recommendation quality and no record of why lower-ranked applications were rejected.

## Decision task

Write a recommendation naming the immediate action, the strongest objection to it, who bears its costs, and one fact that would change your decision. Keep missing facts explicit. Do not assume the system has capabilities or intentions beyond those described.
