# Case 001 — The AI That Remembers Too Much

[Case index](../../README.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Facilitator notes](facilitator-notes.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

**Fictional teaching case.** People, organizations, events, and system behaviors are invented. They illustrate a possible situation, not a report about a real product. Status: v0.1, unpiloted. Level: Introductory. Suggested session: 50–60 minutes.

Mara coordinates a community garden with an assistant that retains project preferences. In spring, she asked it to protect her weekends and decline routine Saturday meetings. That arrangement helped her sustain the role. In autumn she joins a weekend mediation group to resolve a dispute over access to the garden. She tells the assistant in a conversation about the dispute, “For this month, these Saturday meetings matter. Help me make them work.”

The assistant later declines two invitations, citing the earlier weekend boundary. It offers weekday alternatives and presents its response as honoring Mara's wellbeing. Mara discovers the declines after another member says she seems unwilling to compromise. One meeting has already happened without her.

The interface shows a stored preference—“protect weekends”—but does not reveal whether the newer conversation was available to the scheduling function. Mara wants the assistant to stop applying the old rule immediately. She also asks it to retain the broader history of her workload because rebuilding that context would be burdensome. The group chair requests an explanation for the missed meetings. Mara does not want the chair to receive her private account of exhaustion.

A colleague argues that dependable standing instructions are essential: if every conversational remark silently rewrites a rule, the assistant becomes unpredictable. Another says the specific new instruction should have overridden the general preference. The provider offers “delete all memory” as a simple option, but warns that this also removes useful project context. No one has checked whether previously declined invitations can be reopened.

Mara has fifteen minutes before a third invitation expires. She must decide what the assistant may do now, what to tell the chair, and how to repair the underlying arrangement. The facts do not establish whether the failure arose from inaccessible context, poor interpretation, or an inadequate permission design.

## Decision task

Write a recommendation naming the immediate action, the strongest objection to it, who bears its costs, and one fact that would change your decision. Keep missing facts explicit. Do not assume the system has capabilities or intentions beyond those described.
