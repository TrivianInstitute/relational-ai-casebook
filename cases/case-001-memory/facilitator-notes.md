# Facilitator notes — The AI That Remembers Too Much

[Scenario](scenario.md) · [Questions](discussion-questions.md) · [Concepts](concepts.md)

## Session and learning aim

Learners should distinguish competing interpretations, reason under uncertainty, and revise a decision after a changed fact. Allow 10 minutes for private reading, 10 for an initial decision, 20 for competing interpretations, 10 for the variation, and 10 for a final response. A 50-minute session can shorten whole-group reporting. Offer text, speech, or paired responses; no personal disclosure or live AI account is required.

## Teaching analysis

Ask groups to separate immediate repair from future design. An immediate plan might stop automatic declines for the relevant dates, ask Mara about the expiring invitation, and draft a minimal correction for the chair. Another defensible plan would suspend all calendar actions until the authority conflict is resolved, accepting a greater coordination cost.

Competing interpretations: the system failed to respect a specific updated intention; the interface failed to distinguish conversation from a persistent rule change; or the institution delegated too much without a clear correction mechanism. These can coexist. Do not assume deleting every memory is either necessary or sufficient: already sent messages and downstream records are separate.

At minute 20, introduce this variation: Mara says she never wants to manage settings. Ask what a usable change process would look like without treating every expression as an instruction. Evaluate whether proposals handle both predictable continuity and contextual change. A Trivian lens treats stored context and current authority as distinct; learners may argue for other designs if they address the conflict.

Watch for a diagnosis of Mara's exhaustion or an assertion about the assistant's motives. Neither is established. Close by requesting a two-part plan: the next fifteen minutes, and the next month.

## Formative assessment

Look for use of the supplied facts, explicit missing information, fair treatment of an opposing argument, attention to affected people, and a revision condition. Do not grade agreement with the facilitator. Compare the first and final recommendation and ask what changed. This case has no canonical answer key or validated scoring instrument.
